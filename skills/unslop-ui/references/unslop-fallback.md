Condensed from the unslop skill in pstack by Lauren Tan (MIT). Rule numbers match the original, so gaps are expected.

### Content

3. **Superficial -ing phrases.** "showing strong momentum", "ensuring a smooth experience", "highlighting key trends". Delete the phrase.

### Language

7. **AI vocabulary.** Crucial, delve, enhance, seamless, showcase, pivotal, vibrant, landscape, testament, elevate, unlock, empower. Use the plain word or cut.
8. **Fancy ways to say "is".** "serves as", "stands as", "boasts", "features". Say "is" or "has".
9. **"Not just X, but Y."** State the point directly.
10. **Rule of three.** Don't force ideas into groups of three. Use the natural number.
11. **Synonym cycling.** Workspace, project and space for the same thing. Pick one name and repeat it.
12. **False ranges.** "From startups to enterprises" where the ends are not on a real scale. Name who it is for.

### Style

13. **Em dashes.** None. End the sentence or use a comma.
17. **Title case.** Use sentence case.

### Filler

23. **Filler phrases.** "In order to" becomes "To". Delete "It is important to note that".
25. **Generic conclusions.** "The future of finance is here." State a fact or cut.

### Plain speech

27. **Say what it does, not how it feels.** "Finance, simplified" names a feeling. Name the mechanism or a number. If the sentence could sit unchanged on another product's page, cut it.
28. **Shorten or split dense sentences.** One idea per sentence.
29. **Active voice.** "Your changes are saved by the editor" becomes "The editor saves your changes".
30. **Cut adverbs.** Seamlessly, effortlessly, quickly, significantly. Cut them or give the number.
31. **Prefer the plain word.** "Utilize" and "leverage" become "use", "facilitate" becomes "help".
32. **Mannered prose.** Aphorisms, rhetorical fragments and figurative verbs. Say the literal thing.
